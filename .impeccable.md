## Design Context

### Users
**Primary:** Financial services executives (CxOs, VPs of Digital, Heads of Customer Service) evaluating boost.ai's conversational AI platform. They receive a unique URL from their account manager after an initial meeting.

**Context:** Viewing on desktop or tablet during work hours, often sharing the link internally with IT/procurement stakeholders. They're comparing vendors, need to understand the platform quickly, and want to see how it maps to *their* specific use cases (banking, insurance, etc.). Time-poor, skeptical of vendor materials, impressed by specificity over generic claims.

**Secondary:** Technical evaluators (architects, developers) who receive the link from the primary user and want to understand integration points, architecture, and data flows.

### Brand Personality
**Trustworthy, precise, quietly confident.**

boost.ai is a Norwegian enterprise AI company serving banks, insurers, and government agencies. The brand is NOT flashy startup energy — it's the calm authority of a company that handles 10M+ conversations/year for regulated industries. Think Scandinavian design principles: functional elegance, restraint, every element earns its place.

**Emotional goals:** Confidence (this platform is enterprise-grade), clarity (I understand exactly what it does), specificity (this was made for MY company, not a generic demo).

### Aesthetic Direction
**Visual tone:** Clean, structured, professional — but not sterile. The purple (#59195d) is deep and authoritative, the green (#36b595) is action-oriented and fresh. White backgrounds keep it breathable. Information density is high but organized through progressive disclosure.

**Theme:** Light mode. This is a B2B sales tool viewed in office environments during daytime. Dark mode would feel wrong for the audience.

**References:** Stripe's documentation (clean information hierarchy), Linear's marketing site (precise, confident product showcase), Vercel's dashboard (functional elegance).

**Anti-references:** Generic SaaS landing pages with hero metrics, gradient text, and "trusted by" logo bars. Anything that screams "AI startup." No blue. No cyan. No neon accents. No glassmorphism.

**Colors that MUST be used:** Purple #59195d (primary brand), Green #36b595 / #208269 (action/positive), White backgrounds. These are non-negotiable brand colors from boost.ai's Frontify brand portal.

**Colors that MUST be avoided:** Blue (any shade — this is NOT a generic tech company), cyan, neon anything.

### Design Principles

1. **Progressive disclosure over information dump** — Start simple, reveal complexity through interaction. The orchestrator card leads to a modal, which leads to architecture diagrams, which lead to individual node details. Never show everything at once.

2. **Specificity over generics** — Every piece of content should feel like it was built for THIS prospect's company. Dynamic company names, industry-specific agents, volumes pulled from their actual data. No "Lorem ipsum" energy.

3. **Structure communicates competence** — The visual architecture of the guide (flow diagrams, hierarchical agent layouts, systematic categorization) mirrors the structured, systematic nature of the platform itself. The medium IS the message.

4. **Restraint is confidence** — No decorative elements that don't serve a purpose. No animations for animation's sake. Every visual choice should make the prospect think "these people know what they're doing" — not "these people hired a good designer."

5. **Interactive, not passive** — This replaces a PDF. Every section should invite exploration. Clicking, expanding, drilling down. The prospect should feel like they're learning the platform, not reading about it.
